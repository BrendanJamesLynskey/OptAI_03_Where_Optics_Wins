# Optical Technology for AI Processing 03 — Where Optics Could Win, and the Limits

The conditions under which optical arithmetic beats digital, a map of where it wins, which AI operations fit, memory and Amdahl, precision for inference and training, the 1/d scaling argument for very large models, and the niches where the case is strongest.

Topics: Win conditions, Amdahl, Memory wall, 1/d scaling, Latency, Scorecard.

**Live site:** https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/

Part of the [Optical Technology for AI Processing series](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/Optical_Hub_AI_Processing). This is mostly a literature deck: every number is cited on the slide to a paper, datasheet or public company source with a link, or computed live by the deck's interactive; company claims are labelled as the company's own and illustrative coefficients as illustrative. Fourier optics, optical training and device physics are explained in [Fourier Optics for Inference](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/LLM_Hub_Fourier_Optics_Inference), [Fourier Optics for Training](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/LLM_Hub_Fourier_Optics_Training) and [Silicon Photonics Fundamentals](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/Optical_Hub_Silicon_Photonics), and linked, not repeated. Every concept used here is explained, with links, in the [series glossary](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/Optical_Hub_AI_Processing/#glossary).
